About me

I like infrastructure that nobody has to think about.

Seven years in, my favorite compliment is still "I forgot that system existed" — it means the automation is doing its job.

My story

I started as a Linux sysadmin at a retail company, patching servers by hand and learning the hard way what happens when nobody documents the network. That job taught me to respect the boring parts of infrastructure — backups, patching, access control — because they're the parts that turn into 3 a.m. calls when ignored.

From there I moved into site reliability engineering, where I built an observability and on-call practice from nothing for a 30-engineer org. Then cloud infrastructure — migrating a monolith off bare metal onto AWS — and now platform engineering, building the self-service systems that let other engineers ship without waiting on me.

What ties it together is a bias toward systems that fail loudly in staging and quietly in production. I'd rather spend a week hardening a pipeline than spend a weekend firefighting one.
